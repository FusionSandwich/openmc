STATIC WORK ACCOUNTING
OpenMC sets initial_subdivisions=96, max_refinement_levels=7, and requires
refinement stability. At refinement level L, the solver allocates and evaluates
(96*2^L + 1) function samples and the same number of derivative samples over the
complete bounding-box ray interval. It then scans every subinterval for sign
changes and derivative brackets, bisecting roots and stationary points. Even a
two-level stable result starts with 97+193 global function samples and the same
number of global derivative samples, before bisection/refinement work.

The standalone compiled-surface microbenchmark measured 413.299408 ns per
synthetic-helical evaluate and 421.32076 ns per normal call, demonstrating that
individual spline evaluation is not itself a multi-millisecond operation. The
repetition of those operations across a global interval and across repeated
OpenMC surface-distance calls is the dominant design problem.

DIAGNOSIS
CONFIRMED: the generic production path invokes the correctness oracle and its
global interval scan. Local arithmetic tuning cannot plausibly close a 197x
matched-mesh gap. The replacement must spatially reject most of the surface,
solve only local parametric patches, and make production_global_reference_calls
exactly zero.
